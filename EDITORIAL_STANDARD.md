# The Verifier Editorial & Verification Standard

Version 1.0 · August 22, 2026

This is a public-facing standard. It contains no credentials, private moderation systems, confidential workflows, or protected infrastructure details.

## 1. Core distinction

The Verifier treats the following as different categories of material:

1. **Primary record** — an original law, filing, dataset, transcript, scientific paper, official statement, video, image, court document, public record, or other first-order evidence.
2. **Reported news** — factual reporting produced by a newsroom or journalist.
3. **Analysis** — interpretation that explains significance, causation, context, or likely implications.
4. **Editorial / opinion** — the named author's argument, judgment, or viewpoint.
5. **Advocacy** — material created to persuade readers toward a particular action, policy, movement, or position.
6. **Unresolved claim** — an allegation or assertion that has not yet been sufficiently established.
7. **Automated output** — ranking, filtering, clustering, summarization, or labeling produced by software.

These categories should not be silently collapsed into one another.

## 2. What “verified” should mean

A Verifier interface should not label a claim verified merely because:

- it appeared in an RSS feed;
- multiple websites repeated it;
- a source is famous;
- an automated model scored it highly;
- the claim agrees with an editorial position;
- the claim is emotionally compelling.

A strong verification record should identify, where practical:

- the specific claim being evaluated;
- the original source or primary record;
- independent corroborating evidence;
- publication and event dates;
- material contradictions;
- unresolved uncertainty;
- correction history;
- the basis for the final status.

## 3. Verification statuses

Recommended public labels:

- **Confirmed** — supported by sufficiently strong evidence and independent corroboration.
- **Supported** — substantial evidence exists, but meaningful uncertainty remains.
- **Disputed** — credible sources or evidence materially conflict.
- **Unverified** — insufficient evidence is available to reach a conclusion.
- **False / Contradicted** — reliable evidence directly contradicts the claim.
- **Outdated** — the claim may have once been accurate but no longer describes current conditions.
- **Satire / Fiction / Demonstration** — the item is not presented as factual news.

Interfaces should explain the basis of a status rather than presenting a badge as self-authenticating authority.

## 4. Source inclusion is not endorsement

The Verifier source directory exists to support comparison, provenance, geographic breadth, and access.

An outlet's presence in the directory does not mean:

- The Verifier endorses its editorial positions;
- every article published by that outlet is accurate;
- the outlet is politically neutral;
- the outlet is independent of governments, corporations, donors, owners, or advocacy interests;
- the outlet receives a universal trust score.

Source evaluation is story- and claim-specific.

## 5. Editorial labeling

Opinion and editorial pages should visibly identify:

- that the material is editorial or opinion;
- the author's name;
- the publication date;
- meaningful updates or corrections;
- sources for consequential factual claims when reasonably available.

Strong opinion is permitted. Factual assertions inside opinion pieces remain subject to factual scrutiny.

## 6. Human dignity

The Verifier may strongly criticize conduct, policies, ideologies, political movements, governments, corporations, institutions, leaders, militaries, armed groups, and public figures.

The Verifier does not dehumanize entire populations or treat people as subhuman because of nationality, ethnicity, religion, race, sex, identity, or protected status.

Collective condemnation should not be substituted for evidence about specific actors or conduct.

This standard applies consistently regardless of which country, political faction, religion, ethnicity, or conflict is being discussed.

## 7. Conflict and humanitarian reporting

Coverage of war, terrorism, mass violence, atrocities, displacement, famine, and humanitarian crises should distinguish carefully among:

- confirmed deaths or injuries;
- estimates;
- allegations;
- combatants and civilians;
- government claims;
- armed-group claims;
- humanitarian organizations;
- eyewitness accounts;
- independently verified evidence.

Graphic or traumatic detail should be included only when it materially improves understanding.

## 8. Science, health, and research

Scientific reporting should distinguish among:

- peer-reviewed research;
- preprints;
- observational findings;
- randomized trials;
- laboratory or animal studies;
- expert consensus;
- minority scientific views;
- regulatory decisions;
- policy arguments.

Correlation should not be presented as causation. Absence of evidence should not automatically be presented as evidence of absence, and uncertainty should not be exaggerated into proof of danger.

## 9. Corrections

When a meaningful factual error is discovered:

- correct it promptly;
- preserve enough context to make the correction understandable;
- identify the correction date;
- avoid silently rewriting the historical record when the change materially alters the claim.

Minor typographic fixes do not require a formal correction notice.

## 10. Automated ranking

Automated ranking should be explainable in plain language.

The Verifier should not optimize a news ranking for outrage, fear, anger, sensational punctuation, or ideological reinforcement.

Recommended ranking considerations include:

- freshness;
- source diversity;
- geographic diversity;
- primary-source availability;
- corroboration;
- public consequence;
- novelty without sensationalism.

The Daily Top 30 currently uses freshness, source diversity, regional rotation, and a per-source concentration limit. Its appearance order is not a truth score.

## 11. Youth-facing news

A simple list of blocked keywords is not sufficient to guarantee age-appropriate material.

Youth-facing surfaces should prefer:

- curated source sets;
- age-banded presentation;
- explicit sensitive-content screening;
- clear source labels;
- educator or parent guidance where appropriate;
- safe fallback content when a live feed cannot be confidently classified.

Automated screening should be described as a filter or screening aid, not a guarantee of safety.

## 12. Reader independence

The Verifier should help readers ask:

- What exactly is being claimed?
- Who is making the claim?
- What is the original evidence?
- When did the event happen, and when was this published?
- Is the headline stronger than the underlying evidence?
- Are independent sources actually independent?
- Is this reporting, analysis, opinion, advocacy, or advertising?
- What evidence would change my conclusion?

The purpose of verification is not to replace judgment. It is to improve the evidence available to judgment.

---

**Truth is not a badge. Verification is a process.**
