Editorial & Verification Standard

These rules explain how The Verifier distinguishes discovery, reporting, evidence, analysis, opinion, advocacy, unresolved claims, automated output, and corrections.

Return to The VerifierPlain-text standard
Core rule: a story appearing in a publisher feed, search result, automated ranking, or respected outlet does not by itself make the underlying claim verified.

1. Keep categories distinct

  • Primary record: original law, filing, dataset, transcript, paper, official statement, image, video, or other first-order evidence.
  • Reported news: factual reporting produced by a newsroom or journalist.
  • Analysis: interpretation of significance, context, causation, or implications.
  • Editorial / opinion: the named author's judgment or argument.
  • Advocacy: material intended to persuade toward an action, policy, or position.
  • Unresolved claim: an allegation not sufficiently established.
  • Automated output: ranking, filtering, clustering, summarization, or labeling produced by software.

2. Verification means evidence, not repetition

Where practical, a strong verification record should identify the specific claim, original source or primary record, independent corroboration, publication and event dates, meaningful contradictions, unresolved uncertainty, correction history, and the basis for the final status.

Recommended statuses

  • Confirmed
  • Supported
  • Disputed
  • Unverified
  • False / Contradicted
  • Outdated
  • Satire / Fiction / Demonstration

3. Source inclusion is not endorsement

The Global Source Directory supports access, comparison, provenance, and geographic breadth. Inclusion does not mean The Verifier endorses an outlet's editorial positions, considers every story accurate, or assigns the publisher a universal trust score.

4. Editorial labeling and evidence

Opinion pages should visibly identify themselves as opinion/editorial material. Strong opinion is permitted, but consequential factual assertions remain subject to factual scrutiny. When reasonably available, important factual claims should be accompanied by source or evidence references.

5. Human dignity

The Verifier may strongly criticize governments, policies, political movements, corporations, institutions, militaries, armed groups, leaders, ideologies, and documented conduct. It does not dehumanize entire populations or treat people as subhuman because of nationality, ethnicity, religion, race, sex, identity, or protected status.

Collective condemnation should not substitute for evidence about specific actors or conduct. The rule applies consistently regardless of the country, faction, religion, ethnicity, or conflict involved.

6. Conflict and humanitarian reporting

Coverage should distinguish confirmed casualties from estimates; allegations from established facts; civilians from combatants; government claims from armed-group claims; humanitarian reporting from eyewitness accounts; and independently verified evidence from assertions by interested parties.

7. Science, health, and research

Scientific coverage should distinguish peer-reviewed studies, preprints, observational findings, randomized trials, laboratory or animal research, expert consensus, minority scientific views, regulatory decisions, and policy arguments. Correlation should not be presented as causation.

8. Corrections

Meaningful factual errors should be corrected promptly, with enough context for the reader to understand what changed. Material corrections should identify the correction date rather than silently rewriting the historical record.

9. Automated ranking

The Verifier does not optimize news ranking for outrage, fear, anger, sensational punctuation, or ideological reinforcement. Preferred considerations include freshness, source diversity, geographic diversity, primary-source availability, corroboration, public consequence, and novelty without sensationalism.

10. Youth-facing news

Automated keyword filtering must never be described as a guarantee that every open-web destination is safe or age-appropriate. Youth-facing surfaces should favor curation, age bands, transparent outbound sources, and adult guidance where appropriate.
